# Getting 501(c)(3) Status for The Education Foundation

A step-by-step roadmap to obtaining federal tax-exempt status. Fees and requirements verified against IRS sources as of July 2026.

## Phase 1 — Form the legal entity (1–3 weeks)

1. **Incorporate as a nonprofit corporation** in your home state (or Delaware). File Articles of Incorporation with the Secretary of State. The articles **must** contain two clauses or the IRS will reject your application:
   - **Purpose clause**: organized exclusively for charitable and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3).
   - **Dissolution clause**: on dissolution, assets go to another 501(c)(3) organization or the government.
2. **Recruit a board of directors** — minimum 3, and ideally a majority who are unrelated to each other and to the founder. A board dominated by one family is a red flag for the IRS.
3. **Get an EIN** from the IRS (free, instant, online at irs.gov). Do this after incorporating, not before.
4. **Adopt bylaws and a conflict-of-interest policy** at the first board meeting, and record minutes. The IRS application asks about both.

## Phase 2 — Prepare the application (2–4 weeks)

5. **Choose your form.** This is the biggest fork in the road:

   | | Form 1023-EZ | Form 1023 (full) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Fee | $275 | $600 |
   | IRS processing | ~2–6 weeks | ~3–12 months |
   | Eligibility | Projected gross receipts ≤ $50,000/yr for each of the next 3 years, assets ≤ $250,000 | Anyone |
   | Length | 3-page attestation | 20+ pages with narratives, budgets, schedules |

   **Recommendation for TedFun: file the full Form 1023.** Two reasons specific to this organization:
   - A donation platform presumably intends to raise more than $50k/year — exceeding the projection after filing the EZ creates problems.
   - TedFun will make grants to individuals abroad (students in India, given the INR support). Foreign activity doesn't automatically disqualify the EZ, but the full form's **Schedule H** lets you get scholarship/grant procedures pre-approved, which is far stronger footing.

6. **Prepare the Form 1023 package:**
   - Narrative description of activities (how students are vetted, how funds are disbursed)
   - 3 years of projected budgets (or actuals if already operating)
   - **Schedule H** — scholarship/educational grant procedures: objective selection criteria, who selects recipients, how use of funds is supervised, record-keeping
   - Copies of articles, bylaws, conflict-of-interest policy

## Phase 3 — File and wait

7. **File electronically at Pay.gov** and pay the user fee ($600 full / $275 EZ). Non-refundable.
8. **File within 27 months of incorporating** — do this and the exemption is retroactive to the formation date, so donations received while the application is pending become deductible once approved.
9. Respond promptly to any IRS follow-up questions. Approval arrives as a **determination letter** — that's the 501(c)(3) status.

## Phase 4 — After approval (don't skip these)

10. **State obligations**: apply for state income/sales tax exemption, and register for **charitable solicitation** in your state before fundraising. Because TedFun solicits donations online nationwide, multi-state registration (or the Unified Registration Statement) may eventually be needed — start with the home state and states where donations concentrate.
11. **Annual IRS filing**: Form 990-N (< $50k receipts), 990-EZ, or 990. **Miss it 3 years in a row and the status is auto-revoked.**
12. Give donors written acknowledgments for gifts of $250+.

## ⚠️ Two issues specific to TedFun's model — address before launch

- **Donor-designated students.** If a donor gives money earmarked for one specific named student, that gift is **not tax-deductible** and can jeopardize the exemption — it's treated as a gift to an individual, not a charity. The fix (used by every child-sponsorship charity): the foundation must retain **full discretion and control** over funds; donors may express a *preference* for a student, but the board/committee makes the final allocation using objective criteria. The terms of service, donation flow copy, and donor receipts should say this explicitly. This should be reflected in the product UX.
- **Sending money to India.** A US public charity granting to individuals or organizations in India must maintain control and documentation over foreign disbursements, and on the India side, FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act) governs who can receive foreign funds — organizations need FCRA registration; scholarships to individuals are treated differently but still regulated. Get an hour with a nonprofit attorney who handles US–India grantmaking before the first cross-border disbursement.

## Timeline and cost

- **Realistic total timeline:** ~2 months of prep + 3–12 months of IRS processing for the full 1023.
- **Total hard costs:** roughly $600–$1,000 (state filing + IRS fee), plus attorney fees if used (commonly $2–5k, worthwhile given the cross-border piece).
